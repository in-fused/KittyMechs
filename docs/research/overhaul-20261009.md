## Recommendation

Rebuild the player experience around authored feline animation, responsive traversal, paired combat, and a coherent, densely populated destructible city. Keep the existing Three.js/R3F browser foundation and useful multiplayer safeguards provisionally. Replace the gameplay and art systems that prevent those features from working convincingly. Evaluate WebGPU on the same representative scene before migrating the renderer.

The published prototype falls below the requested visual and gameplay standard. Earlier technical checks established narrow correctness; they did not establish enjoyable climbing, convincing cat combat, rich city presentation, or realistic collapse. More generated pictures, larger block counts, and more passing assertions cannot substitute for those outcomes. Approval must include ordinary play footage and player evaluation.

This is a production plan for the complete game: four distinct mechs, three substantial environments, Co-Op missions/checkpoints, PvP/PvPvE, traversal and structural damage. The first integrated comparison scene is a tool for selecting and proving the replacement systems. It is not a reduction of the requested final scope to one street or a curated climbing demo.

## 1. What the supplied examples actually demonstrate

### TitanCraft: custom gameplay over an ordinary renderer

I opened [Attack on TitanCraft](https://attack-on-titancraft.netlify.app/), inspected its controls, started Free Roam, viewed the rendered scene and observed one brief jump input. I did not join its advertised multiplayer room, assess sustained swinging/combat feel, or measure frame time. The scene has a coherent voxel aesthetic, nearby city density, readable combat UI and traversal-oriented rooftops. It is a useful reference for focused game design and presentation.

Its public HTML imports Three.js **0.160.0** and PeerJS **1.5.4**. The served [engine source](https://attack-on-titancraft.netlify.app/js/engine.js) constructs `THREE.WebGLRenderer`; it also implements voxel geometry and face shading. Its [game source](https://attack-on-titancraft.netlify.app/js/game.js) separates pilot, enemies, missions, audio, effects and networking modules. These are custom game systems built on Three.js, rather than a new graphics API written from scratch. The controls describe independently aimed grapple anchors, reel/boost actions, speed-dependent blade attacks, enemy weak points and attack warnings. Publicly served code is not evidence of a reuse license.

[Nick Saraev's post](https://x.com/nicksaraev/status/2108604020814233606) attributes the game to Opus 5.5 and reports about an hour and less than $25. Those are the creator's claims; I have not verified the development history, cost or one-prompt conditions. The playable result is evidence that a sharply focused browser game can look cohesive. It does not independently establish the budget for a photorealistic multiplayer feline-mech game with structural destruction.

### Tokyo: a city content pipeline, not just a rendering toggle

[Tokyo](https://jeantimex.github.io/tokyo/) visibly renders dense varied buildings, station infrastructure, rooftop detail and consistent lighting. I saw the city while tile loading was still in progress; this was visual inspection, not a loading or performance benchmark.

The [project README](https://github.com/jeantimex/tokyo) describes offline compilation of PLATEAU buildings, OpenStreetMap roads and GSI elevation/aerial data into streamed binary tiles. Its [renderer source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jeantimex/tokyo/main/src/main.js) explicitly uses `THREE.WebGLRenderer`. The [package manifest](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jeantimex/tokyo/main/package.json) uses Three.js 0.186.1 and Takram atmosphere/cloud/geospatial packages. This is not the version currently installed in KittyMech.

The source is MIT, while map and image data retain separate terms, including OSM's ODbL obligations. A city viewer does not supply our climb contacts, structural supports, combat or multiplayer. We should adapt the offline compilation approach and art discipline, with a purpose-built game city and licensed content. Copying the entire viewer would transfer substantial unrelated geographic complexity.

### Blender + Meshy + Unreal posts

The accessible portion of [Rewind's workflow post](https://x.com/rewind02/status/2108195576206488058) names Opus 5.5, Blender, Unreal and Meshy, assigning tools different jobs. Both are creator-reported model workflows, not controlled comparisons with KittyMech or independently playtested AAA benchmarks. No quality scores from third-party AI-game lists are treated as evidence here.

### WebGPU and Strata

WebGPU is useful for GPU compute, large particle workloads and renderer redesign. It does not author a good cat gait, place a convincing neighborhood, or design a satisfying tackle. Three's [r180 WebGPURenderer source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r180/src/renderers/webgpu/WebGPURenderer.js) includes a WebGL2 fallback. [R3F supports asynchronous renderer initialization](https://r3f.docs.pmnd.rs/api/canvas), so keeping R3F does not exclude WebGPU. Browser availability and secure-context requirements remain relevant ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebGPU_API)). Existing materials, postprocessing and custom passes need compatibility work; changing one constructor is not a complete migration.

The specific Strata package I could verify is [@stratavcs/mcp](https://registry.npmjs.org/@stratavcs%2Fmcp). Its README describes repository, cooking, deployment and GPU verification tools; its metadata tags Unreal, WebGPU and SimplyStream. Version 0.2.0 is marked `UNLICENSED`, and its advertised GitHub repository returned 404. This establishes a build-orchestration surface, not an openly licensed drop-in Three.js engine. It does not establish that every Strata mention in the supplied posts refers to this package.

[SimplyStream's official site](https://simplystream.com/) describes client-side WebGPU delivery through an Unreal Engine fork, alongside optional cloud delivery; its [build guide](https://simplystream.com/docs/build-guides) documents the SDK workflow. The npm Strata package's verification marker names SimplyStream, making a connection plausible, but its full architecture and current access terms were not established. This route would reintroduce Unreal authoring/builds. It is researched as an alternative, not selected under the user's browser-first/no-Unreal direction. Vendor FPS marketing is not a KittyMech performance result.

Anthropic's [Opus 5.5 release](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5) verifies that model and reports improved game creation in its own tests. The [model overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview) read during this research listed Fable 5.1; I could not independently confirm a Fable 5.5 entry. That metadata uncertainty does not invalidate the supplied games. There is no equal-budget model comparison on this repository, so model superiority or insufficiency is not an established diagnosis.

## 3. Architecture and engine decision

| Option | Fit for this game | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Three.js + R3F + Rapier | Existing code and network authority are reusable; full freedom for feline traversal and structural systems. | Preferred baseline for the overhaul. React owns menus/state boundaries; mutable frame simulation stays outside React rerenders. |
| Three WebGPU/TSL | Potential advantage for dense city effects and compute. Requires asset/pass migration and measured fallback behavior. | A bounded comparison branch using identical content. Adopt only after visual and performance evidence on target browsers. |
| PlayCanvas | Browser editor and animation state graphs; an [official Editor MCP](https://developer.playcanvas.com/user-manual/editor/mcp-server/) can manipulate scene/project assets. [Animation graph docs](https://developer.playcanvas.com/user-manual/animation/anim-state-graph-assets/) document authoring facilities. | Strong alternative if editor-based scene/animation production demonstrably improves iteration. No account, integration or port has been tested. |
| Babylon.js | More integrated browser engine, with [Havok integration](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/physics/usingPhysicsEngine). | Viable alternative; changing engine still leaves feline rigging, game design and city production to do. No migration selected. |
| Godot | Strong desktop authoring. [Current web export docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html) specify the Compatibility/WebGL2 renderer and web threading constraints. | Not a shortcut to browser WebGPU graphics. Consider only for demonstrated workflow gains. |
| Unreal + SimplyStream/Strata | Native-engine authoring and potential browser GPU delivery. | Conflicts with the current direction and requires a separate engine decision. No reinstatement or installation. |

Use a **custom game layer**, not a custom graphics engine: fixed-step authoritative movement/contact/combat/structural state; frame-rate presentation; explicit action and contact events. Keep network versioning, actor life/epoch ownership, input acknowledgement, cancellation and loading guards unless a targeted audit finds a flaw. Decompose the oversized simulation and actor-pose pipelines behind stable interfaces instead of deleting all accumulated safeguards.

Proposed interfaces, not existing implementations: `TraversalState` owns contact/support and carrier motion; `ActionState` owns attack/stance phases; `AnimationState` consumes accepted speed/turn/contact/action data; `StructureState` owns health/support/ruins; `RegionState` owns content readiness and snapshot revisions. The renderer must not decide damage, grapple ownership or climb success.

## 4. Mechs and movement: production specifications

The hero model and game model need the same approved design. Build one editable source asset per chassis, with close/mid/far LODs, consistent scale, material maps and attachment sockets. Choose detail for its value at gameplay-camera distance. Armor seams, curved shells, pistons, exposed mechanisms, moving shoulder assemblies and distinctive heads are geometry/material work; a high-detail title image cannot supply those automatically.

### Four identities

These proposals retain the existing roles while expanding their visual and mechanical differences:

| Chassis | Silhouette and feline gait | Quadruped combat/traversal | Upright identity and defense |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pounce — skirmisher | Athletic panther proportions, flexible waist, long counterbalancing tail; compress/extend gallop and powerful rear-leg launch. | Aimable leap, grapple pounce, claw combo, rebound off a wall. | Mobile boxer/grappler; long lateral steps, claw parry and throw. |
| Bastion — guardian | Broad tiger shoulders, heavy paws and layered armor; deliberate planted walk and forceful short bound. | Shoulder tackle, pin, slab-breaking landing and brace. | Wide weight-bearing steps, shield bracing, shove/slam and protected ally revival. |
| Lynx — recon | Narrow frame, long hind limbs and compact tail; quick directional footwork and extended bounds. | Wall ricochet, flank strike, evasive roll/dive and chase. | Fast fencing-like steps, precision offense and mobility defense. |
| Caracal — medic | Tall ears/sensor vanes, distinct forearm devices and springy rear stance; controlled hops and stable contact poses. | Leap to an ally, repair while gripping, interruption strike. | Support stance, directional protective field and recovery/rescue actions. |

Weapons, mass, hit reach, recovery and defense must match each silhouette. Share protocols and tools, not identical pose curves and recolored shells.

### Rig and animation contract

Author scapula motion, pelvis/spine flex, toes/claw deployment, tail balance, neck/head tracking, hind-leg folding and rigid armor-panel articulation. A cat control rig can start from Blender's [Rigify quadruped/cat facilities](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/addons/rigging/rigify/basics.html?highlight=pose+mode), then needs customization for mechanically plausible biped conversion. Export a simplified deform/panel rig with baked GLB clips; keep the editable source/control rig separately.

First clip set: idle/search/crouch; directional starts/stops and turns; walk/trot/bound; leap anticipation/launch/air/landing; wall catch, ascend, descend, lateral movement, corner transition, ledge rise; quadruped↔upright conversion; upright start/long-stride/turn/stop; pounce/tackle/contact/paired pin/claw/throw/release; stagger, fall, recovery and downed. Each clip declares stride distance, foot-contact windows, action/contact times and valid transitions. A shared action vocabulary does not mean every chassis uses the same timing.

Drive cycle phase from actual traveled distance and direction. Retain foot plants through blends; use IK to correct contact error, not to manufacture an entire performance. Allow real body roll, shoulder transfer and spine extension using an envelope designed for those motions. Transformation changes center of mass, camera framing, step reach and available actions, with a blended collision representation.

Author root-motion curves, but let swept authoritative movement constrain them. Warp within bounded reach to the accepted destination; blend into a blocked/recovery action if a wall prevents the move. The animation never teleports the collider or passes through geometry to complete a clip. Preserve buffered jump and air control. Quad form has longer, higher, responsive leaps with rear-leg anticipation; upright form has stable large strides and purposeful upper-body movement.

[BlueTwelve's own account of Stray](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=367025) describes a cat animator and programmer refining jumps and transitions using real-cat references. It supports investing in movement choreography and responsiveness together. It does not supply a reusable Stray rig or permit copying its animation files.

## 5. Universal climbing and feline combat

### Surface traversal

All buildings must expose climbable gameplay geometry throughout their façades and rooftops. Extend the same contract to structural props, towers, bridges and usable rubble. Do not introduce an artist-curated whitelist of grab markers. Query nearby collision geometry, normals and material/support data at the actual paw region. A finite-sampling coverage test is useful but cannot prove every point in continuous space.

Jump/pounce toward a feasible surface automatically transitions to claw catch when collision, direction and reach agree. An explicit input should control release or preferred behavior without requiring a fiddly held-C handshake for every wall. Support ascent, descent, sideways movement, convex/concave corners and ledge crossings. Continuously calculate feasible support and center-of-mass placement. The solver may prevent penetration, unsupported poses or physically impossible overreach; it must not reject a normal façade just because no marker was placed there.

Claw contacts damage local surface material, release small chips and leave scrape marks. Heavy landings damage the touched roof/ledge proportionally to accepted impact and material resistance. Damaging support can cause grip loss; animate paw slip/regrip or a fall rather than levitating on a removed face. Visible meshes, collision owners, climb contacts and structural health derive from the same compiled building description.

### Combat action graph

Replace the airborne-only strike carrier with actions that can begin from a grounded crouch, run, wall or air. Flow: anticipation → commit → contact/miss → coordinated reaction → recovery/cancel. Add leap pounce, shoulder tackle, downward dive, forepaw swipe, hind-leg kick, pin-and-claw, escape and throw. Aim and momentum affect outcome; clear timing and feedback make the result legible.

Paired interactions require authority-owned attacker/victim IDs, life epochs, accepted start tick, relative anchor and allowed transitions. Validate reach/line of travel before locking a pair. Shape-sweep both bodies, keep them out of buildings, and release on death, disconnect, interruption or destroyed support. Prevent two attackers acquiring the same victim. Give victims counter/escape windows and teammates interruption opportunities; do not turn PvP into an unavoidable repeated stun.

Combine authored paired clips with contact adjustment. Use target-specific reactions for light enemies, large braced opponents and other mechs. Large foes may deflect or resist a pin. Audio, sparks, local deformation, short camera impulses and carefully bounded visual hit pauses reinforce contact. The server simulation continues during visual hit pause.

Enemy design must expose these mechanics: scout flankers, braced bruisers, rooftop hunters and ranged suppressors with distinct warning, windup, attack, recovery, retreat and support behavior. Repath when structure changes. [Navcat](https://github.com/isaac-mason/navcat) is an MIT floor-navigation candidate; wall/roof connections and tactical behavior remain custom systems. [Overgrowth's source](https://github.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth) is an Apache-2.0 procedural combat reference; its commercial art is separately restricted and is not a browser-ready feline kit.

## 6. Dense environments and structural destruction

### City content and streaming

Create a modular architectural kit with real proportions and a consistent material language: foundations, structural frame, floor slabs, façades/windows, roof profiles, stairs, bridges and street dressing. Offline compile authored district plans plus constrained variations into regional files. Validate road clearance, terrain alignment, roof accessibility, supports and collision ownership during compilation.

Rainline Reach becomes an extensive coastal metropolis with mixed-height blocks, quays, transport corridors, alleys and industrial waterfront. Ember Quarry uses dense foundry structures, conveyors, gantries and stepped cliff works. Frostline Basin uses station compounds, buried service passages, elevated connections and weather-sheltered routes. All three need deliberately composed encounter spaces and traversal routes across their full playable extents; changing fog and ground color is not a distinct map.

Near regions receive full interactive geometry; medium regions simplified coherent geometry; far regions silhouette/instance impostors. Physics/traversal data must be ready ahead of player reach, including maximum leap speed and network correction margin. Preload a predictive ring. If a region is not ready, prevent entry at a coherent boundary or keep the prior valid region; never introduce an obstacle inside a moving actor. Decorative details may stream progressively after stable structural collision is established.

Use instancing/batching, texture atlases/trim sheets, mipmapped compressed materials and spatial visibility culling. Introduce authored roof details with thickness and clean separation, shared support ownership and correct damage removal. Diagnose artifacts under the actual camera before assuming a universal depth fix.

### Destruction must change the world

Use a structural graph: material cells or pre-fractured clusters connect to columns, slabs and foundations. Impacts reduce local health and support strength; graph changes identify unsupported components. Detach components into bounded rigid chunks with force direction, mass and rotational impulse. Damage on one side influences which supports fail; gravity and remaining support determine the subsequent motion. This is a designed approximation to structural behavior, not a promise of engineering-grade fracture simulation.

Define coverage explicitly: façade, floor/roof slabs, load-bearing frame and destructible props. Persistent state includes missing components and settled ruins. Fine dust/chips are cosmetic; dangerous large chunks have authority-owned collision. When active-body capacity is reached, sleeping debris becomes stable ruin geometry with preserved collision instead of restoring an intact building or deleting important cover. Aggregate distant/offscreen collapse while retaining coherent structural outcomes. Physics budgets are selected by profiling, not copied from the current 24-body experiment.

Send compact regional structural changes, support revisions and relevant fragment transforms. Reconstruct static geometry from a pinned building catalog and seed. Late joiners receive the same damage/ruin checkpoint; clients cannot invent collapse independently. Streaming must retire stale climb/roof contacts and AI navigation together with a support change.

[Three-piñata](https://github.com/dgreenheck/three-pinata) is MIT geometry slicing/fracture tooling and expects separate physics integration and suitable input meshes. It is useful for an offline fracture bake, not a complete structural-collapse system. [ShatterVox](https://github.com/AgusDropOut/ShatterVox) demonstrates WebGPU voxels, Rapier and worker-based structural processing, but its [license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AgusDropOut/ShatterVox/main/LICENCE.md) permits non-commercial use only with attribution. It is a study reference; its code is not admitted to a commercially viable KittyMech build. No unrestricted reuse claim is made for it.

A fully voxel-based city is a legitimate alternative to the graph/cluster approach. Compare a deformable voxel building against a detailed modular building using the same climb/damage scenario. Select based on visual quality at cat-camera distance, collision coherence, memory, authoring and network cost. The default proposal preserves realistic architectural surfaces using pre-fractured structural clusters; fine voxel cubes need not dictate the game's appearance.

### Weather, effects and sound

Weather should affect visibility, lighting, surface wetness/snow, ambient audio and selected tactical areas without hiding weak geometry. Use shared weather state, local scalable particles, restrained exposure and cohesive sky/light settings. Tune anti-aliasing, contact shadows, material roughness and reflection treatment before adding stronger bloom or color filters.

Area effects need readable boundary, activation, active and decay stages plus distinct harm/help colors and sound. Repair, suppression, electrical arcs and impact shockwaves should have clear gameplay ownership. Reactions to weather and damage are visual/audio production work as well as simulation. [Bruno Simon's portfolio source](https://github.com/brunosimon/folio-2025) is a useful MIT reference for deliberate scene/game-loop/physics/effect integration and included Blender authoring, not a ready-made mech combat package.

## 8. Co-Op and progression remain first-class

Keep Co-Op as a continuous shared expedition: stages with clear objectives, encounter escalation, safe checkpoint activation, revives/rescues, complementary chassis abilities and meaningful powerups. Author routes that reward vertical access and coordinated attacks. Mission progress, structural damage and encounter state must survive late join and checkpoint respawn.

Authority owns pickup spawning/consumption, grapple locks, revives and structure transitions. Use deterministic spawn candidates with controlled randomness and sensible cooldowns; avoid unreachable pickups in unloaded or destroyed regions. ## 10. Acceptance bar and critic authority

Proposed targets below are not measured achievements. Use Taylor's detected GTX 1070 Ti as the first baseline candidate, record browser/resolution and actual measurements, and select a representative friend's PC/network before treating targets as binding. No constant small-step oscillation, rigid torso or forced pause on normal ledges. Show all four classes at ordinary camera distance in both forms.
- **Combat:** a player can deliberately pounce from ground or wall, make contact, see a coordinated victim reaction, choose a follow-up and escape/cancel. Misses and defended attacks have distinct readable outcomes. Test mech-vs-mech and multiple enemy masses.
- **Climbing coverage:** all authored buildings and structural props publish support/material/contact data. Impossible reach/absent material is explained by geometry, not an arbitrary building whitelist.
- **Destruction:** impact/claw damage is visible at contact; losing support changes geometry and traversal. Major rubble remains represented after settling, streaming and late join. Opposite-side impacts have coherent different outcomes, subject to remaining supports and gravity.
- **City quality:** normal views contain convincingly populated near/mid/far architecture with consistent scale/materials and deliberate detail. No empty traversal expanses masked by fog, coplanar roof flicker or obstacles spawning inside a player.
- **Performance:** initial proposal: 1080p medium on an agreed desktop, median frame interval at or below 16.7 ms and p95 at or below 20 ms during traversal/combat; investigate any hitch above 50 ms. - **Fun and visual review:** uninterrupted normal-play video plus hands-on evaluation can reject a technically correct milestone. If the reviewer/user still sees clunky traversal, weak impact or unconvincing art, the milestone remains a prototype and the next task fixes that specific failure.
