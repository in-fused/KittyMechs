# Physical city rubble milestone

Physical rubble is a **default-off private experiment**. The critic denied default-on adoption after the full suite exposed breached-doorway and support-loss regressions. Normal construction retains the earlier cosmetic fracture path, with no native rubble allocation or dynamic controller/query participation. This is an iteration of KittyMech-Web only; the Unreal reference remains read-only. This document is not a public release approval.

When a trusted authority explicitly constructs `Simulation` with the sixth argument `{experimentalPhysicalRubble:true}`, eight convex fragments replace each accepted eligible Rainline structural cell, starting the next simulation tick in both dedicated and externally stepped worlds. The fifth injected-Rapier argument stays unchanged. The strict boolean is copied at construction and cannot be activated by input, join packets, settings or mid-match toggles. The authority owns gravity, collision and transforms. Experimental rubble blocks movement, projectiles and Simulation clearance/visibility queries; committed native contact responses can push it. Guests display admitted transforms on the same buffered clock as actors, with quaternion interpolation and no independent physics or ballistic duplicate.

The snapshot carries an explicit authority mode. Admission rejects malformed modes, physical pieces in cosmetic mode and mode changes within a match. Presentation retains that mode even when physical arrays are empty. Normal play uses the layout-owned cosmetic fragment pool; an empty physical array never selects the cosmetic path. Break events and geometry share the buffered presentation clock, and copied event vectors survive sender mutation.

The owner retains at most 24 bodies, evicts complete oldest components, and retires them after eight simulation seconds. The accepted-break queue also retains at most three components, bounding new allocation to 24 bodies per step; excess accepted damage still leaves permanent holes. These are limits on body count/allocation, not a measured browser frame-time guarantee. Pause freezes training physics and extraction retires rubble. Source body/collider identity and finite transforms are checked before publication, with failed ownership poisoning subsequent physics/publication and best-effort cleanup retaining unknown residual bodies for retry.

Controller adaptation preserves the existing no-rubble query tuple and owned-actor classification policy. Foreign dynamic bodies remain excluded through precomputed JavaScript handle sets; query callbacks do not read Rapier state. Temporary dynamic impulses/mass settings restore their captured values. The installed native automatic impulse pass is temporarily disabled during owned-rubble movement queries and replaced only after an accepted carrier movement commits. Climb and mantle keep their autostep-disabled policy. A bounded upward-grounding repair starts with actual owned-rubble contact and requested and accepted positive vertical movement, then retains that proof only through consecutive accepted local rises. Remote rubble, exact-zero ceiling movement and descent cannot grant this continuation; life, hull and climb changes clear it. The experimental external blocked-takeoff failure remains recorded below.
