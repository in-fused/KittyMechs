# Architecture

React 19 hosts menus and HUD. React Three Fiber 9 / Three.js render the scene; React Three Rapier 2 supplies the browser physics world. Zustand stores UI state and settings. TypeScript is shared across client, server and tests. Vite creates a static browser build.

## Simulation boundary

`shared/simulation.ts` owns movement, collision, projectiles, AI, damage, resources, respawns and objective completion. It receives validated inputs, advances at 60 Hz and emits immutable snapshots. Each browser authority uses the single Rapier world provided by its R3F Physics provider. A Node arena creates one world per room. Guest clients have a separate local world containing terrain/cover for camera obstruction only; it never determines damage.

Rendering reads authority data outside React. Body and camera use one coherent frame-cached presentation timeline: local authority reads the live 60 Hz simulation and presents one physics tick behind; guests retain eight pooled snapshots per actor and present about 100 ms behind with bounded clock correction. It never extrapolates through unconfirmed cover. Position, heading, velocity, climb progress and action time use that same sample. Death, respawn and discontinuous travel reset immediately. Gait phases follow visible distance with authored duty-based paw support/release, reducing slow-walk claw dragging and stopping gait progression during a stalled connection. This is presentation smoothing, not client prediction or server rewind. Animation mixers, camera and effects update through `useFrame`. Roster changes are checked at 5 Hz, DOM HUD at 10 Hz and network snapshots at 20 Hz. A maximum of 192 active projectiles, 96 recent events, 12 persistent area fields and quality-dependent particle pools bound combat allocation. Effects trim immediately when graphics budgets shrink; solo pause freezes their lifetime clock.

Form requests carry an explicit desired stance. Authority checks grounded/clearance conditions, commits a 0.7-second transition, gates attacks/jumps and updates rounded hull height. The taller collision envelope is retained until lowering finishes. Replicated stance/time drive the same clip phase for late observers. Camera height follows the visible transition, while aim acquisition and projectile collision use the conservative hull. In-flight damage remains the value selected at launch.

Shield/heal/snare fields have authoritative owner, radius, birth and expiry timestamps. Simulation integrates exactly the active portion of each tick, applies mode-specific friendliness and cover checks, and removes fields when owners leave or die. The renderer consumes field/event footprints without deciding damage. Terrain-conforming field geometry is memoized by scalar placement values and disposed on expiry. On supported roofs/cargo tops, oriented polygon clipping prevents rims/fills from draping down the building. Destroyed cargo invalidates support selection. EMP arcs render only actual accepted hit events, with a fixed 16-target pool.

`shared/weather.ts` derives gradual fronts, lighting/visibility and analytic wind drift from the map seed and shared room clock. Clients generate bounded weather particles and a procedural cloud sky from those values, without weather-specific network packets. Reduced flashes zero lightning. Weather does not currently alter traction, AI sight, or combat damage.

Held climb input carries no requested anchor or position. Authority selects an admitted facade from `shared/traversal.ts`, checks reach/facing/headroom and the landing, commits grab/cling/climb/mantle progress, and moves through the existing character controller. Each chassis has a distinct ascent rate; grip costs 8 energy and attached movement/cling consumes 7/3 per second without regeneration. The 2.6 m player/3.2 m Sentinel climb hull retains its floor, clears the complete roof before the mantle, and remains for 0.3 seconds after release while the animation fades. Landing occupancy is checked continuously. Snapshots deep-copy nested contacts; late observers sample the same progress. The actual interpolated camera is sphere-swept from a safe focus after recoil, avoiding near-wall and roof clipping.

Stable authored cover IDs own bounded HP and destruction timestamps in complete snapshots. Projectile collision selects the winning actor/cover hit. Rocket splash resolves against intact geometry before damaged cover bodies retire exactly once. Destroyed IDs survive chunk reloads; terrain grading stays unchanged. Navigation occupancy and bot paths invalidate on retirement. Guests filter visible instances by a stable destroyed-ID signature and remove exact tracked cover colliders rather than rebuilding at snapshot frequency. One cosmetic debris instance pool is limited to 32/64/96 fragments by quality and expires independently of gameplay collision. Fragments check their current horizontal position against actual roof/cargo support and fall beyond the edge. Each theater currently admits one retaining wall and two forward-roof cargo banks.

Co-op relay/core captures advance a deep-copied shared checkpoint. Default late joins and respawns use it as the center of the existing bounded safe-placement search; living players are not relocated. Optional roof objectives and extraction never overwrite it. PvP/PvPvE retain their original spawn rules. An objective can progress only once per tick even when several allies hold E; separate relays can advance concurrently.

Six bounded supply entries use a map/room-salted random stream. Validated ground positions respect navigation, static hull clearance, objectives, other caches and living actors. A grounded living pilot must hold E within three meters and have actual line of sight. Beneficial claims are atomic: repair gives 25% maximum armor and visible co-op teammates within 10 m also benefit; energy adds 45 up to 100; coolant removes 65 heat and resets missile readiness. A full cache remains unclaimed. After 55–75 seconds, a new location/kind is selected, with bounded delayed retries if placement is unavailable. Guests send only ordinary input; supplied state/health/location fields are ignored. The HUD uses a read-only static ray check to describe blocked/full interactions accurately.

## Three transports, one ruleset

| Transport | Authority | Intended use |
|---|---|---|
| Solo training | Local browser | Immediate play without networking |
| Browser squad | Host browser, PeerJS/WebRTC data channels | Invite URL on static public hosting |
| Dedicated arena | Node `server/index.ts`, WebSocket | Independent local/LAN authority or future Internet server |

Guest inputs are sent at 30 Hz. The authority owns spawn positions and damage; guests cannot supply positions or health. Nonfinite values and invalid enums are rejected, axes are clamped and stale input releases after 300 ms. The Node server bounds room size, payload length and incoming rate, disconnects stalled joins and retires empty rooms. Browser hosting is a friends-only prototype, without authenticated identities or malicious-host protection.

Default late joins and respawns check the assigned slot, then 32 deterministic candidates in four rings within 20 m. Placement checks live hulls (including retained tall retraction), streamed terrain/static cover and world limits. Conservative outer-box clearance also catches fully contained static obstacles. A downward whole-hull terrain cast finds a supported floor within the existing 0.55 m autostep budget, followed by full static/occupancy containment checks. This permits gentle slopes without skipping terrain, walls or ceilings. All KCC movement excludes disabled corpse colliders, including deaths accepted earlier in the same tick. If no candidate is safe, the actor remains pending with its existing collider disabled and retries after 0.5 seconds. Clear assigned slots are preserved; synthetic explicit spawn inputs retain their tool/test contract. Bot navigation separation includes live pilots.

Snapshots are interpolated on remote clients; there is no client prediction or server rewind yet. Reliable data channels can accumulate latency under poor links. PeerJS cloud provides signaling, while game data travels between peers. A TURN relay is the highest-priority networking addition for networks where direct WebRTC fails. Host migration and background-tab simulation are not implemented; the host must stay active.

A temporary Cloudflare Quick Tunnel now exposes the independent Node authority through HTTPS/WSS for tonight's sessions. That route does not require direct peer connectivity or a host browser tab, but the PC/server/tunnel must stay running. The Node-served HTML marks its same-origin arena with a meta tag; **Host squad** then uses WebSockets automatically. Static hosting uses browser invites unless `VITE_ARENA_URL` is configured. The public service is a development tunnel with a changing hostname, not durable production infrastructure; see [Cloudflare Quick Tunnels](https://developers.cloudflare.com/tunnel/get-started/quick-tunnels/).

The public helper can run a frozen reviewed release under `.sites-runtime/live-<commit>`, selected by `live-build.json`. `tools/prepare-live-release.ps1` requires committed reviewed source and copies server/shared/compiled assets with a hash receipt. This prevents subsequent development builds from overwriting files served to active friends. After release, refresh the pointer and restart the arena while retaining the existing tunnel; preserve the previous frozen build for recovery.

## World and assets

`shared/maps.ts` supplies three authored objective networks, spawns, patrol routes, roads, cover placements and landmarks. `shared/world.ts` grades roads/building pads and deterministically generates 64 m chunks. Both rendering and collision use 32-segment terrain tiles. At rest, the renderer displays 9 or 25 detailed chunks according to quality. A forward strip up to 24 m ahead preloads visible terrain/props while retaining the full current neighborhood, bounded to 16 or 36 tiles respectively. A worker computes exact shared terrain positions, continuous normals and UVs; a 96-entry CPU cache avoids repeated terrain preparation. The coarse underlay retains ground until a detailed tile is ready. All solid props, including tree trunks, remain visible at every graphics quality; only noncollidable foliage uses density culling. Physics streams a one-chunk radius around pilots/bots and retains enemy spawn districts. Landmark boxes share exact render/physics descriptors; cover boxes encompass their solid visible walls. Actor collision uses rounded circular hulls sized for each chassis, and eight human spawn slots have 4.6-meter spacing. Projectile rays now hit the actual form collider. Crosshair acquisition uses a finite-cylinder proxy. Tails/antennae are cosmetic; precise articulated contacts remain production work.

`shared/navigation.ts` uses a bounded cached grid with rotated cover bounds, corner and slope checks. Per-bot private memory stores visible/heard locations, last-seen expiry, patrol progress and paths. Roles select flanks, guard locations, retreats or mortar windup. `shared/vertical-navigation.ts` adds bounded roof-plane routing around actual static/retired cover. A Scout follows a real approach, grip/climb/mantle and roof pursuit, then exits through supported edge movement; an Anchor holds its authored roof home. No fabricated stair or roof teleport is used. `shared/encounters.ts` shares exact SKY UPLINK geometry/interaction rules between authority and guests. A named living guardian anywhere blocks capture; local enemies, stance, height, reach and station line of sight are also checked. Its one-time EMP suppresses enemy weapons for eight seconds within 60 m of the actual antenna emitter, while allowing movement; 200 salvage and visible allied energy support do not gate the ground mission. The renderer only displays replicated states; it cannot decide enemy damage. A short input latch preserves quick keyboard/touch action taps across render, physics and 30 Hz transport sampling. Form intent lasts through the bounded transition/retraction window and cancels on blur, pause or death. Accepted shot/ability/hurt/death timestamps drive additive authored animation; rejected requests never generate recoil. Colliders disable on death while the corpse root continues falling against actual world support until respawn.

`useGLTF` caches compressed GLB geometry. Static prop primitives render in instanced batches. Batch disposal releases instance buffers while preserving shared model geometry. Cloned actor materials, animation mixers, terrain geometry, weather geometry and physics objects have explicit teardown. Model cache lifetime is the page lifetime.

High/ultra add bloom and vignette; lower presets skip them. DPR, shadow maps, vegetation density, chunk radius and effects budgets are real preset parameters. Asset paths are local and stable. Runtime generation produces layout descriptors, not art assets or remote generation requests.

## Module map

`src/game/Session.tsx` connects inputs and authority. `network.ts` owns transport lifecycle. `entities/Actors.tsx` renders rigs and hit/death feedback. `camera/CameraRig.tsx` handles follow, obstruction and crosshair-to-muzzle aiming. `world` streams geometry and beacons. `effects` uses bounded instanced combat particles and weather. `src/ui` owns deployment, settings, HUD, touch input and results. `tools` handles source export, texture conversion, model compression and receipts.

Cosmetic ocean and mountain backdrops use shared/scenery.ts. Water stays west of the square movement boundary plus 32 m; mountains begin beyond its corner radius plus 32 m. They provide no collider or gameplay support and cannot cover legal actor/camera positions.
