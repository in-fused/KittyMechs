# Chassis articulation reassessment 001 — October 10, 2026

**Recommend one underlying redesign: a connected structural chassis with two-axis shoulder/hip yokes, separate fixed bearing races and moving hubs, and armor dressed around their measured joint corridors.** Retain the oblique side-contact choreography as an unqualified requirement; stop changing root angles, paw targets or pose constants to compensate for the chassis. This is a read-only design reassessment, not another candidate, implementation authorization or approval. Gameplay gates remain incomplete and counts unchanged.

Read the sidepin author plan/interface contract, relevant local pose/support construction, final repair contract and selected exact moving witnesses. No source edits, render, geometry/physics job or external mechanical research was performed. The author-plan text remains an initial plan; the final repair contract owns the stopped result.

## What the failure establishes

First-contact attacker FR upper armor vertex **9766** newly enters closed shoulder mantle by **.203558818 m**. At settle, FR bearing vertex **785** enters its mantle by **.188814968 m**. Victim HR bearing vertex **13777** enters pelvis by **.056419346 m**, and FR bearing vertex **869** enters chest by **.053999200 m** after the recorded modest far-limb motion. Each selected point was outside the target at rest and has changed relative deformation. Bearing names do not define a permitted seat.

The useful static partner clearance, topology, floor and fixed-link results remain evidence, but do not qualify articulation. Identical affine deformation at one static pose proves only unchanged relative geometry there; it cannot establish a permanent rigid assembly or a moving bearing seat. The failure does not prove every side-contact design impossible. It establishes that the retained closed shapes and present part ownership lack demonstrated operating joint space.

## Build the mechanism before its armor

Use the original detailed Pounce as the visual reference and preserve its masters. Keep the panther skull/muzzle/optics, folded ears, canopy/pilot, broad scapular silhouette, flank lamellae, rear turbines, copper rails, curved limb plates, full palms/guards/digits/claws/dewclaws, hydraulic appearance, articulated tail and material families. **Preserve their identity and detail, not the literal solid joint shapes that caused the failure.** Changed dimensions, pivots, owners and action consumers must be recorded honestly.

The thoracic frame carries the shoulder yokes; the pelvic frame carries the hip yokes, turbines and pelvic support rail. An articulated, connected spine joins those frames through declared joints. Each frame's permanently rigid members have one consistent owner across every motion. Do not leave a keel assigned to an independently moving spine joint while relying on a static chest/keel insertion. Whole-body translation/orientation belongs to native authority; internal motion uses joint transforms with fixed link endpoints.

For each shoulder and hip, model a **fixed outer yoke/race → abduction/adduction hub → sagittal flexion link**. Locate their axes and actual bearing centers explicitly. A whole decorative solid sphere rotating with the upper link inside a closed torso is not this mechanism. Split fixed races, moving hubs, collars and seals into geometrically bounded members; give each the transform it actually follows. Journals contact their declared seats with explicit surface/clearance limits. Exterior armor remains outside other material volumes. Permanent same-owner material joins may be recorded as a solid assembly; moving occupancy requires a real seat, never a broad rest-overlap exemption.

Shoulder mantles and thigh cowls become a **fixed dorsal/flank arch plus a separate moving proximal cap**. The arch bolts to the structural frame. The cap follows its hub/link and ends before adjacent joint travel. Curved upper/shin plates remain detailed but terminate outside knee/wrist corridors. Knee hinges and wrist pitch/roll joints get the same fixed-seat/moving-member treatment. Hydraulic barrels and rams have separate endpoints, bounded telescoping travel and owned receiving interiors. Do not transfer an entire cowl to the limb simply to make an overlap disappear.

At the tail base, the fixed pelvic collar and first moving tail member are separate; each later segment begins at its predecessor endpoint. Scales belong to their actual segment and stop outside the next joint corridor. Floor and pelvis clearance include the complete tail, not its centerline.

Side/ventral support rails anchor through visible brackets into the thoracic/pelvic **frame**, not a moving limb cover or an armor plate treated as a structural seat. Record the material section and uninterrupted seat-to-frame path. Soles remain finite parts of the complete paw assembly. Added supports participate in mass, floor, self/partner clearance and native bounds; they cannot be hidden carriers.

## Finite joint domain and proof boundary

Define one connected motion domain from the actual required **quad gait stride, braking/turning, stance transition and side-contact reach/withdrawal**. State each joint's axes, limits, coupled exclusions and hydraulic travel. Do not claim an arbitrary Euler box, and do not derive the chassis only from five paired endpoints. The operating volume is the union of complete moving members over those paths, including interpolation and recovery. Design the fixed frame/armor around that volume with a declared clearance margin and minimum remaining wall/section rule. If necessary travel removes the recognizable shoulder/hip mass or breaks the structural path, report that blocker rather than widening a cutter repeatedly.

Before future whole-chassis authoring is authorized, the design should specify one representative forequarter and one hip mechanism with their complete knee/wrist/paw chain, seats, swept volumes and load path. That is the smallest useful mechanical review unit; a partner claw-gap solve is not. Then an authorized implementation would need to demonstrate full-body gait and side-contact corridors, including spine/tail interactions, before paired clips or runtime migration. This note starts none of that work.

Required receipts are a persistent part-to-owner/joint graph; fixed/moving seat geometry; bidirectional closed occupancy including noncrossing containment; wall/section minima; conservative continuous clearance; fixed endpoint/length checks; and mass counted once through explicitly owned assemblies. Support needs finite contact polygons and feasible reactions under stated mass/friction assumptions. Geometry closure alone supplies neither strength nor pressure.

Native authority later needs conservative pose-aware whole-body envelopes, actual position/quaternion/linear/angular velocities and contact/support impulses. The renderer follows the same joint state with one pose owner. Added joints require explicit clip/consumer migration and independent audits of old gait/transform actions; bone-name compatibility cannot transfer acceptance. Preserve reciprocal contact epochs, escape/cancel/life/team rules and network reconstruction from the prior design contract.

Feasible joint range, structural margins, COM/inertia/reactions, continuous cancel corridors, source silhouette and native solver cost remain **UNKNOWN**. Full commercial CAD validation is unnecessary for this game design recommendation; a declared simplified load model and honest geometry/contact proof are necessary. Normal-camera acceptance must still show planted paws, visible weight transfer and a readable low restraint. Passing ownership or collision tests cannot approve weak gait or hovering contact.
