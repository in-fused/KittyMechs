## What the actual images establish

The board's industrial vocabulary is coherent without every item sharing one silhouette. The freight cart reads through its bed rails, cargo cases, tiller and four exposed wheel hubs; the repair trolley has a different, taller stack of open trays and a small bottle. The cable reel's winding and circular flanges distinguish it from the compact anchored winch. The manifold's three red handwheels, posts and gauge provide recognizable controls. Tank caps, straps and the dark bund frame make the pressure bundle legible. Generator controls, edge hardware, cargo restraints, fence feet and mesh, hatch slots, bench slats and the charger cable are visible rather than existing only in part counts. These details are sufficient to suggest manufactured service equipment; they are not evidence of mechanically functional devices.

Ochre, cobalt, restrained red, graphite and pale steel sit together consistently. Yellow rails/straps and red valves provide useful accents, while the black reel/tire forms separate rubber/cable from painted housings. Labels help explain use at this source-camera distance. Fine label text, wheel lugs and fence grid may disappear at normal player distance; no normal-camera readability has been measured.

The finish is the main limitation. Large paint panels, crates and slabs read unusually clean and smooth. Weathering is barely visible in these lighting conditions; the distinction between old working metal and newly painted equipment is weak. The broad box and pallet faces still have a simplified, almost model-kit appearance. Small repeated fasteners help close inspection but do not establish rich surfaces at game distance. This does not invalidate the silhouettes or require another source bake; it prevents treating these images as final environment-quality proof.

The finite West loading study gains an understandable service story: generator/manifold/reel grouped to the left, restrained cargo near the warehouse, trolley and freight cart toward the marked access edge, and a floor hatch. Objects remain individually readable and do not form an undifferentiated clutter pile. However, the placement is very orderly, with a broad empty floor and a nearly blank warehouse wall. It looks like a staged local arrangement. It establishes neither lively city density nor the intended ordinary camera experience. There is no mech/player reference in these pixels, so apparent visual scale cannot qualify encounter readability or full-body passage.

Unseen areas include object backs/undersides, other lighting, actual streets beyond this finite slab, distant silhouette/horizon, East/Core/staging population and full warehouse frontage. Motion, camera occlusion, crowding, destruction and guest rendering were not observed.
