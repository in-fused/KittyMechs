Camera 058 is a source candidate for the prototype's third-person movement camera. It has not been built, tested or played by the author. The 057 result remains limited acceptance; the selected boards are not event-aligned and do not establish the cause of every missing-body frame.

Two source defects justify the change. The old pose displaced the look target by `sin(pitch) * pitchReach`, while the boom followed a different equation. At absolute pitch 0.3 and above, fitting stopped entirely. Its ideal envelope fit also did not test whether opaque render-only objective geometry lay between the actual eye and the body. Objectives render the beacon pedestal and signal box without native colliders.

The prototype now uses a body-centered orbit. With back vector B=(sin(yaw)cos(pitch), -sin(pitch), cos(yaw)cos(pitch)) and camera-up vector U=(sin(yaw)sin(pitch), cos(pitch), cos(yaw)sin(pitch)), pivot=body focus + boomHeight*U, eye=pivot + distance*B, and look=pivot - lookAhead*B. Body-center composition therefore remains below aim across pitch, while the optical direction is exactly -B. Public look controls and movement yaw are unchanged. The existing rendered center-ray to native cover and muzzle-aim calculation remains intact. Fitting now applies throughout prototype pitch; legacy standard/fallback pose behavior remains.

The completed current-generation camera owner supplies the actual head, FL_upper and FR_upper world positions. These are three rig witnesses rather than skin-pixel visibility certificates. Hidden ancestors exclude an anchor. Replacement cleanup and current-frame ownership remain enforced; no fabricated registration or native actor proxy is used.

Beacon components explicitly register their opaque, non-skinned meshes. Registration traverses that component once, cleans up on unmount and does not adopt the global scene. Each sight query checks current-generation ownership, ancestor/material visibility and current world transforms. A conservative world AABB skips remote meshes before exact triangle raycasts; a camera inside a registered AABB is conservatively rejected. HTML labels are outside this registry. Beacon geometry, opacity, native collision and mission behavior are unchanged.

After the existing native camera sweep, the resolver checks actual envelope projection, empty native camera sphere and at least two visible rig-anchor rays (all available anchors if only one is present). Native cover and beacon triangles are separate checks. If the current eye qualifies, it is retained. Otherwise, six bounded alternatives use the desired orbit with body-scaled lateral/up offsets. Every alternative passes the existing native sweep and occupancy test and preserves optical yaw/pitch. If none qualifies, the previous safe eye is restored and `bodySight.constrained` remains true. `bodySight.contained` reports projection alone; `anchorsClear` reports the separate witnesses. These diagnostics cannot certify rendered skin visibility.

The resolver evaluates at most seven positions, three anchors each, six additional native sweeps and seven occupancy checks. The first admitted candidate stops the search. Render queries visit only the explicitly registered beacon meshes, with AABB rejection before triangle testing. No global scene traversal, collider exclusions, pitch waiver, hull change or body hiding was introduced. Pair focus/yaw biases remain; pair projection includes both envelopes but sight witnesses cover the local body only.

Tests supplied for root execution exercise actual Three projection across moving positions/yaw/pitch, exact optical direction, unchanged legacy pose, impossible-fit reporting, current owner bone transforms/visibility/generation, render mesh transforms/material visibility/retirement, a lateral beacon avoidance path, and an actual Rapier wall forcing a constrained camera without penetration. They are unrun. Root should run the exact focused command in the handoff, then its serial build and independent public review.

Limits remain near cover, inside beacon volumes, missing known rig anchors, and obstacles with no admitted bounded view. The AABB origin guard can conservatively constrain a hollow mesh. The registry covers beacons rather than all opaque scenery. Native clearance can contract the camera enough to crop the body; that result is reported rather than certified. Candidate transitions can visibly correct the eye when an obstruction appears. Nothing here qualifies full gameplay, surface contact, gait, performance or multiplayer mission completion.
